\title{When Does Direct Collateral-Risk Certification Improve Slice-Aware Intrusion Response?}
\author{[Author names and affiliations to be supplied]}
\markboth{IEEE Transactions on Network and Service Management (submitted)}{Direct vs. False-Positive Certification of Intrusion Response}
\maketitle
\begin{abstract}
Mitigating a flagged user equipment harms benign users when the detector
errs, and in a sliced 5G user plane the harm depends on the slice and
the action. We ask when certifying each slice's action-induced
service-level-agreement (SLA) violation risk directly, using
counterfactual replay and Learn-then-Test risk control, admits more
attack mitigation than certifying per-slice false-positive rates. We
prove that for a work-conserving water-filling scheduler,
traffic-removing actions cannot harm non-acted users, so false-positive
certification already bounds collateral. Relocating actions, such as
redirection to a scrubbing service, break this bound. In a fluid
simulator with a detector under domain shift, certified methods kept
every budget in all splits. In eight pre-registered independent
replications, the improvement of direct over false-positive
certification was not confirmed: the mean change in delivered attack
fraction was -0.030, with 95\% interval {[}-0.071, +0.011{]}. With
redirection, a post-hoc large-sample analysis showed the false-positive
criterion accepting policies that exceed their budgets, although no
finite-sample violations were observed; direct certification remained
valid. The certificate should be chosen by action type.
\end{abstract}
